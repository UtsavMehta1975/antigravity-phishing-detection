❓ Judge Q&A Cheat Sheet (Basic to Advanced)

          
Click any question below to reveal the winning defense response for judges.


          
          

            

              Q1 (Basic): Isn't Gmail / Outlook already doing this?
              ▼
            

            

              Gmail and Outlook are great for known spam, but have critical blind spots against modern evasion: 1) Embedded QR codes (Quishing) bypass text-only scanners. 2) Links behind Cloudflare CAPTCHAs cause automated bots to give up and falsely report sites as clean. 3) Legacy filters are black boxes that don't explain why something was blocked. AntiGravity solves this with multi-modal detection, provenance tracking, and an explainable dual-layer interface.
            

          


          
          

            

              Q2 (Basic): What is Quishing and why is it dangerous?
              ▼
            

            

              Quishing is QR Code Phishing. Attackers send an email saying 'Your MFA session expired, scan this QR code with your phone to renew.' Corporate computers have firewalls and endpoint protection, but employee personal phones do NOT. Quishing tricks victims into moving from a protected workstation to an unmonitored personal phone. AntiGravity uses Computer Vision to decode QR codes in attachments before mobile engagement occurs.
            

          


          
          

            

              Q3 (Intermediate): What is the UNKNOWN_GUARDED state?
              ▼
            

            

              When a link hides behind Cloudflare Turnstile, hCaptcha, or 403 challenge walls, security scanners cannot see what is on the other side. Legacy tools make one of two mistakes: they mark it clean (false negative) or block everything blindly (false positive). AntiGravity introduces the formal UNKNOWN_GUARDED state: we alert the analyst that an evasion wall is deliberately concealing the destination, in compliance with NIST SP 1270 Knowledge Limits.
            

          


          
          

            

              Q4 (Intermediate): How do you catch HTML/SVG smuggling without downloading malware?
              ▼
            

            

              HTML/SVG smuggling never downloads malware over the wire. It uses JavaScript inside the browser to assemble binary files inside RAM memory and triggers an automatic download via hidden anchor tags. AntiGravity uses static AST and regex inspection to catch the assembly blueprint (Blob creation, URL.createObjectURL, atob decoding) before the browser ever executes it.
            

          


          
          

            

              Q5 (Advanced): Why use a Directed Evidence Graph (networkx) instead of a simple probability score?
              ▼
            

            

              In modern SOC incident response, a single score like '87% Risk' tells an analyst nothing about how the threat entered or what intermediate nodes are infected. By modeling scans as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) using networkx, we trace the full provenance chain, compute the exact Critical Risk Path in glowing red, and allow analysts to inspect individual nodes (MIME headers, entropy, IP reputation) in under 3 seconds.
            

          


          
          

            

              Q6 (Advanced): How do you detect zero-day URLs not on any blacklist?
              ▼
            

            

              Blacklists only catch yesterday's attacks. AntiGravity catches zero-days using structural and behavioral signals: 1) RDAP domain registration age (<14 days triggers high anomaly). 2) Shannon entropy (detects algorithmically generated domain names). 3) Brand typosquatting / Levenshtein distance on unverified ASNs. 4) AI cognitive pretext analysis. Even with zero blacklist matches, these combined signals correctly push the risk score into PHISHING.
